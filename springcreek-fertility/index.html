Who we are

An independent, patient-centered fertility practice serving all of Ohio

SpringCreek Fertility was founded in 2014 by Dr. Jeremy Groll with a simple commitment: to provide reproductive medicine that is both clinically excellent and deeply human. Today, our reproductive endocrinology team cares for patients across the Dayton, Columbus, and Cincinnati metros — and from neighboring communities in Indiana and Kentucky.

A reproductive endocrinologist is a physician who completed advanced training in Reproductive Endocrinology and Infertility (REI) — the field focused on the hormones and conditions that affect the ability to conceive. As an independent practice, we are able to keep our care personal, our explanations clear, and our decisions centered on what is right for you.

We believe family looks different for everyone. Whether you are an individual, a couple, an LGBTQ+ family, or a single parent by choice, you will find inclusive care and compassionate support at every step.

Answers to common questions

Frequently asked questions

Clear, direct answers to what patients across Dayton, Columbus, and Cincinnati ask us most.

Where can I find a fertility clinic near Dayton, Columbus, or Cincinnati, Ohio?

SpringCreek Fertility has three Ohio locations: Dayton (7095 Clyo Road, Centerville/Dayton 45459), Columbus (6760 Avery-Muirfield Drive, Suite A, Dublin 43016), and Cincinnati (9313 Mason Montgomery Road, Mason 45040). We also welcome patients from nearby communities in Indiana and Kentucky.

How much does IVF cost in Ohio?

Costs depend on your treatment plan and the medications you need. We offer up-front, itemized pricing and a financial counselor who reviews your insurance benefits with you. In vitro fertilization (IVF) cycle pricing starts in a defined range, with medications billed separately. Visit our fertility treatment costs page or ask our team for a current, personalized estimate.

What happens at my first fertility appointment?

Your first visit begins with a thorough review of your reproductive health, medical history, and goals with a board-certified reproductive endocrinologist. Your physician then recommends any helpful testing and explains your options in clear, plain language so you can decide on next steps together.

Is SpringCreek Fertility LGBTQ+ inclusive?

Yes. We provide inclusive care for diverse family structures, including LGBTQ+ individuals and couples, single parents by choice, and people building families in many different ways. Our team helps explore options such as IUI, IVF, donor eggs or sperm, and gestational carriers. Learn more on our LGBTQ+ family building page.

Does SpringCreek help single people who want to become parents?

Yes. We welcome single individuals pursuing parenthood. Depending on your goals, your physician may discuss donor sperm with intrauterine insemination, in vitro fertilization, donor eggs, or working with a gestational carrier — along with counseling and legal resources.

Where can I freeze my eggs in Dayton, Columbus, or Cincinnati?

Egg freezing (oocyte cryopreservation) is available at all three SpringCreek Fertility locations. It preserves mature eggs now so they can be stored for a possible future pregnancy. See our egg freezing page to learn more.

What is the difference between IUI and IVF?

Intrauterine insemination (IUI) places prepared sperm directly into the uterus around ovulation. In vitro fertilization (IVF) retrieves eggs and combines them with sperm in a laboratory, after which a resulting embryo may be transferred to the uterus. Your physician will explain which approach best fits your situation.

Do I need a referral to see a reproductive endocrinologist in Ohio?

Many patients schedule directly without a referral, though some insurance plans request one. Our team can help you check your plan’s requirements before your visit.

When should I see a fertility specialist?

A common guideline is to seek an evaluation after 12 months of trying to conceive, or after 6 months if you are age 35 or older. Consider reaching out sooner if you have irregular cycles, known reproductive conditions, prior pregnancy loss, or questions about preserving fertility. This guidance is general and is not a substitute for personalized medical advice.

Does insurance cover fertility treatment, and are financing options available?

Coverage varies by plan. SpringCreek participates with many insurance plans, and a financial counselor will review your benefits with you. Financing and payment-plan options are available to help make care more manageable. Explore financing options and understanding your insurance benefits.

We know cost is one of the biggest sources of stress on a fertility journey. That is why SpringCreek offers up-front, itemized pricing and a dedicated financial counselor who will review your insurance benefits and walk you through your options.
