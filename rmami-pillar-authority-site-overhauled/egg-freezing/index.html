Egg Freezing & Fertility Preservation

Egg freezing in Troy, MI, to help keep your future options open.

Egg freezing (oocyte cryopreservation) preserves unfertilized eggs for possible future use. At RMA of Michigan, it can support age-related planning, fertility preservation before medical treatment, gender-affirming planning, or personal timing — serving Troy, Oakland County, metro Detroit, and Ohio.

In brief

What is egg freezing?

Egg freezing is a fertility preservation method in which eggs are collected from the ovaries and frozen for possible future use. The medical term is oocyte cryopreservation. Later, frozen eggs can be thawed, fertilized through IVF (in vitro fertilization), and transferred to the uterus as embryos. Freezing eggs at a younger age can help preserve more options, though it does not ensure a future pregnancy.

Quick answer

At RMA of Michigan in Troy, an egg-freezing cycle typically involves about 10–12 days of stimulation and monitoring, then a short retrieval. Eggs are frozen by vitrification and stored until you are ready to use them.

How it works

The egg-freezing process, step by step

Egg freezing follows the same first half as an IVF cycle — the difference is that eggs are frozen rather than fertilized right away.

Most patients complete the active part of a cycle in about two weeks, then decide when to use their stored eggs in the future.

  1. Testing & consultation

    Ovarian reserve testing (including AMH, anti-Müllerian hormone) and a consultation help set realistic expectations and a personalized plan.

  2. Ovarian stimulation

    Injectable medications encourage the ovaries to mature multiple eggs, typically over about 10–12 days.

  3. Monitoring

    Morning ultrasound and bloodwork visits track follicle growth so medication can be adjusted.

  4. Egg retrieval

    A short, minimally invasive procedure under sedation collects the mature eggs.

  5. Freezing & storage

    Mature eggs are frozen by vitrification, a fast-freezing method, and stored securely until you choose to use them.

  6. Future use

    When you are ready, eggs are thawed, fertilized through IVF, and a resulting embryo can be transferred.

Is egg freezing right for you?

Who may consider egg freezing

People choose egg freezing for many reasons. A consultation and testing help you understand whether, and when, it makes sense for you.

Planning ahead by age

Preserving eggs at a younger age, when egg quality and number are typically higher, to keep future options open.

Before medical treatment

Oncofertility — fertility preservation before chemotherapy, radiation, or other treatment that may affect fertility.

Gender-affirming planning

Preserving fertility before gender-affirming care, with inclusive, individualized guidance.

Personal & career timing

Keeping options open while focusing on education, career, relationships, or other priorities.

Before certain surgeries

When a planned procedure or a condition such as endometriosis may affect the ovaries over time.

Banking before IVF

Collecting eggs across cycles to build a reserve before pursuing IVF later.

Individualized care

What shapes your egg-freezing plan

Age and ovarian reserve are the biggest factors in how many eggs a cycle may yield and how those eggs may perform later. Because not every egg survives thawing and fertilization, some patients choose more than one retrieval cycle.

Egg freezing preserves options, but it does not ensure a future pregnancy. Your physician will discuss what is realistic based on your testing, age, and goals, along with related paths such as embryo freezing or donor options.

Frequently asked

Common questions about egg freezing in Michigan

When should I consider egg freezing?

Many patients explore egg freezing when they want to preserve options before fertility declines with age, before a medical treatment that may affect fertility, before gender-affirming care, or for personal timing. Ovarian reserve testing helps personalize the timing.

How does egg freezing work?

After testing and consultation, injectable medications stimulate the ovaries to mature several eggs. The eggs are collected during a short retrieval procedure under sedation, then frozen by vitrification and stored. Later, eggs can be thawed, fertilized through IVF, and transferred.

How many eggs should I freeze?

The number depends on age and ovarian reserve, because not every egg survives thawing and fertilizes. Some patients complete more than one retrieval cycle to bank additional eggs. Your physician can discuss a target based on your goals.

How long can frozen eggs be stored?

Eggs can be stored frozen for years. Storage involves an ongoing fee, and you decide when to use, continue storing, or discontinue storage. The team will explain storage logistics and paperwork.

Does freezing my eggs ensure a baby later?

No. Egg freezing preserves options, but a future pregnancy is not certain. Results depend on your age at freezing, the number and quality of eggs, and the later IVF and transfer steps. Your physician can discuss what is realistic for you.

How much does egg freezing cost in Michigan?

Costs vary by medication needs, the number of cycles, and storage fees, plus your insurance benefits. RMA of Michigan provides a written estimate and reviews financing and medication savings before you begin.

Is egg freezing available for gender-affirming care?

Yes. Fertility preservation, including egg freezing, can be part of planning before gender-affirming care. The team provides inclusive, individualized guidance.
