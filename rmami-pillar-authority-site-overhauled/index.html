In brief

What is RMA of Michigan?

RMA of Michigan is a fertility clinic in Troy, Michigan, led by board-certified reproductive endocrinologists. The practice provides IVF (in vitro fertilization), IUI (intrauterine insemination), egg freezing, donor egg IVF, PGT-A genetic testing, fertility preservation, male infertility evaluation, and inclusive family-building care for patients across metro Detroit, Oakland County, Michigan, and Ohio.

Quick answer

Founded in 2006, RMA of Michigan combines advanced reproductive technology with a compassionate, individualized approach. Care begins with a consultation and fertility testing, then a treatment plan built around your diagnosis, age, and goals.

About the practice

Compassionate, evidence-informed fertility care in Troy.

Building a family does not always follow a straight line. For more than fifteen years, Reproductive Medicine Associates of Michigan has walked alongside patients navigating that journey — answering questions, explaining options in plain language, and tailoring care to each person’s body, history, and hopes.

The practice was founded in 2006 by Dr. Brad T. Miller and Dr. Lynda J. Wolf, and has since grown to a team of five board-certified reproductive endocrinologists. RMA of Michigan blends technical proficiency with a deeply empathetic approach, personalizing each patient’s experience rather than treating fertility as a one-size-fits-all process.

As a member practice of the Society for Assisted Reproductive Technology (SART) and the American Society for Reproductive Medicine (ASRM), with physicians certified by the American Board of Obstetrics and Gynecology, RMA of Michigan is committed to care that is current, careful, and centered on you.

Fertility services

A full range of fertility treatments and family-building options.

From first-step testing to advanced treatment and third-party reproduction, RMA of Michigan offers care paths for many different needs and goals. Explore the services below to learn what each option involves.

IVF (In Vitro Fertilization)

Eggs are retrieved and combined with sperm in the laboratory, and resulting embryos are transferred or frozen. IVF may help with tubal factors, male factor infertility, diminished ovarian reserve, endometriosis, or unexplained infertility.

IUI (Intrauterine Insemination)

A less involved treatment that places prepared sperm into the uterus around ovulation. IUI may be considered for some ovulation timing needs, mild male factor findings, or donor sperm plans.

Egg Freezing

Also called oocyte cryopreservation, egg freezing preserves unfertilized eggs for possible future use — an option some choose for personal timing, before medical treatment, or for gender-affirming planning.

Donor Egg IVF

Uses eggs from a screened donor and may support intended parents when using their own eggs is not the recommended path. Includes guidance through donor selection and coordination.

PGT-A Genetic Testing

Preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy is an optional screening of IVF embryos that evaluates chromosome number to help inform embryo-selection discussions with your physician.

LGBTQ+ Family Building

Inclusive care that may include donor sperm IUI, IVF, reciprocal IVF, egg freezing, donor eggs, or gestational carrier coordination, tailored to your family-building goals.

Fertility Preservation

Egg, sperm, or embryo freezing to help protect future family-building options — including oncofertility care for patients facing cancer treatment.

Male Infertility Evaluation

Male factor contributes to many fertility journeys. Evaluation includes a semen analysis and, when helpful, coordination with urology to guide next steps.

Gestational Carrier Care

Medical coordination for intended parents working with a gestational carrier, alongside the recommended legal and emotional support.

Fertility Testing & Diagnosis

Ovarian reserve testing (including AMH, anti-Müllerian hormone), semen analysis, and uterine and tubal evaluation help turn uncertainty into a clearer plan.

PCOS & Ovulation Support

Care for polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) and ovulation challenges, including ovulation induction and individualized treatment planning.

Your patient journey

What to expect, step by step.

Fertility care can feel overwhelming at first. Here is a simple overview of how the path typically unfolds at RMA of Michigan — so you know what comes next at every stage.

Bring prior fertility records, recent labs, imaging, any semen analysis results, a medication list, and your insurance card to make your first visit as productive as possible.

  1. Schedule your consultation

    Meet your physician, share your history, and ask your questions. Together you will outline a starting plan.

  2. Fertility testing & diagnosis

    Ovarian reserve testing, semen analysis, and imaging help identify the factors that matter for your plan.

  3. Your personalized plan

    Your care team reviews results with you and discusses treatment options, timelines, and costs in plain language.

  4. Treatment & monitoring

    Begin treatment with close monitoring and support from the clinical team, including morning monitoring when needed.

    From first call to next steps, the team helps coordinate care, finances, and resources along the way.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about fertility care in Michigan

When should I see a fertility specialist in Michigan?

Many people benefit from a fertility evaluation after 12 months of trying to conceive, or after 6 months if age 35 or older. It is reasonable to seek guidance sooner with irregular cycles, known reproductive conditions, recurrent pregnancy loss, donor needs, or a time-sensitive fertility preservation goal.

How much does IVF cost in Michigan?

IVF (in vitro fertilization) costs vary by medication needs, laboratory services, genetic testing, embryo freezing, insurance benefits, and the cycle plan. RMA of Michigan provides individualized financial guidance and written estimates so you can plan before treatment begins.

Where can I freeze my eggs near Detroit?

RMA of Michigan offers egg freezing (oocyte cryopreservation) at its Troy location, convenient to Oakland County and metro Detroit, with a Livonia location planned. A consultation and ovarian reserve testing help determine whether egg freezing fits your goals.

What is the difference between IVF and IUI?

IUI (intrauterine insemination) places prepared sperm into the uterus around ovulation and is a less involved treatment. IVF (in vitro fertilization) retrieves eggs, fertilizes them in a laboratory, and transfers or freezes the resulting embryos. The right option depends on diagnosis, age, and goals.

Does RMA of Michigan support LGBTQ+ and single-parent family building?

Yes. Care may include donor sperm IUI, IVF, reciprocal IVF, egg freezing, donor eggs, and gestational carrier coordination. RMA of Michigan welcomes LGBTQ+ patients, single parents by choice, and individuals and couples building their families.

Does insurance cover fertility treatment in Michigan?

Coverage varies by plan and employer. Some plans include diagnostic testing, medications, or treatment, while others do not. Our financial team helps you verify benefits, review financing options, and explore grants such as the Christen Goff IVF Grant.

What fertility tests are done before treatment?

Common evaluations include ovarian reserve testing (such as AMH, anti-Müllerian hormone, and ultrasound), a semen analysis, and imaging of the uterus and fallopian tubes. Testing helps the care team understand contributing factors and discuss options with you.

How do I schedule a consultation at RMA of Michigan?

Call (248) 619-3100 or request an appointment online. The scheduling team will help confirm the visit type, location, and what records to bring to your first consultation in Troy, Michigan.
