Donor Egg IVF

Donor egg IVF in Michigan, a path to parenthood with screened donor eggs.

Donor egg IVF uses eggs from a carefully screened donor, fertilized through IVF (in vitro fertilization) and transferred as an embryo. It can support intended parents when using their own eggs is not the recommended path, and RMA of Michigan guides you through each step.

In brief

What is donor egg IVF?

Donor egg IVF is a treatment in which eggs from a screened donor are fertilized with sperm in the laboratory, and a resulting embryo is transferred to the intended carrier's uterus. It may be considered for diminished ovarian reserve, age-related changes in egg quality, certain genetic concerns, premature loss of ovarian function, or for some LGBTQ+ and single-parent family-building paths.

Quick answer

Donors are screened with medical, genetic, and psychological evaluation. Intended parents can often choose between fresh or frozen donor eggs and known or anonymous donors, with guidance from the RMA of Michigan team.

How it works

How donor egg IVF works

Donor egg IVF coordinates a donor's cycle with the intended carrier's preparation. Here is the general sequence.

  1. Consultation and planning

    Your physician reviews your history and goals and explains donor options, timelines, and the medical, legal, and emotional steps involved.

  2. Choosing a donor

    You select from screened donors, fresh or frozen, known or anonymous, with support from the care team and any agency involved.

  3. Donor screening

    Donors complete medical, genetic, and psychological screening consistent with professional guidelines.

  4. Fertilization in the lab

    Donor eggs are fertilized with partner or donor sperm, sometimes using ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection), and embryos are cultured.

  5. Uterine preparation

    The intended carrier's uterus is prepared with medication and monitoring for transfer.

  6. Embryo transfer

    A selected embryo is transferred, with any remaining embryos frozen for future use.

Individualized care

What shapes a donor egg IVF plan

Choosing donor egg IVF is a significant decision, and many people benefit from time, information, and counseling support along the way. The team helps you weigh fresh versus frozen eggs, known versus anonymous donors, and how genetic testing fits in.

Outcomes are influenced by embryo quality, the carrier's uterine health, and overall plan, rather than the intended parent's own egg age. Your physician will talk through what is realistic for your situation.

Donor egg IVF involves medical, legal, and emotional planning. Legal guidance is recommended, and this page is educational rather than legal or medical advice.

Cost & coverage

Planning for cost and coverage

Donor egg IVF costs depend on the donor source (fresh or frozen, known or agency), medication, laboratory services, and your insurance benefits.

The RMA of Michigan financial team helps verify your benefits, provide a written estimate, and review financing before treatment begins, so you can plan with fewer surprises. Patients can also explore the Christen Goff IVF Grant and ask about IVF medication savings.

Frequently asked

Common questions

Who might consider donor egg IVF?

It may be considered for diminished ovarian reserve, age-related changes in egg quality, certain genetic concerns, early loss of ovarian function, or for some LGBTQ+ and single-parent paths. A consultation helps clarify whether it fits.

Are donor eggs fresh or frozen?

Both options exist. Frozen donor eggs can offer flexibility and shorter timelines, while a fresh cycle is coordinated with the donor. Your team will explain the trade-offs.

How are egg donors screened?

Donors complete medical, genetic, and psychological screening consistent with professional guidelines. The goal is informed, careful matching.

Can intended parents choose the donor?

Yes. Intended parents typically select from screened donor profiles, with support from the care team and any agency involved.

Is counseling part of the process?

Counseling and legal guidance are recommended for third-party reproduction, helping everyone understand the medical, emotional, and legal aspects.

Does insurance cover donor egg IVF?

Coverage varies widely. The financial team helps verify benefits and review financing before treatment begins.
