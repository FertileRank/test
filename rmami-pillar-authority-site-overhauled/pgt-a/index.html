PGT-A Genetic Testing

PGT-A genetic testing in Michigan, an optional part of IVF.

PGT-A (preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy) is an optional test that checks IVF embryos for the correct number of chromosomes. It is one tool that can help inform embryo-selection discussions, and your RMA of Michigan physician will explain whether it fits your plan.

In brief

What is PGT-A?

PGT-A stands for preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy. During IVF, a few cells are gently removed from an embryo and tested to check whether the embryo has the typical number of chromosomes. Embryos with the expected number are sometimes called euploid. PGT-A is optional and is one of several factors a physician and patient consider together when planning an embryo transfer.

Quick answer

PGT-A does not change an embryo; it provides information. It may be discussed based on age, history of miscarriage, prior IVF, or personal preference. It is not required for every IVF cycle.

How it works

How PGT-A works within an IVF cycle

PGT-A is added to a standard IVF cycle at the embryo stage. Here is how it generally fits in.

  1. IVF through embryo culture

    An IVF cycle proceeds through stimulation, egg retrieval, fertilization, and embryo development to the blastocyst stage.

  2. Embryo biopsy

    A few cells are carefully removed from the outer layer of suitable embryos by the laboratory team.

  3. Embryos are frozen

    Biopsied embryos are typically frozen while testing is completed.

  4. Genetic analysis

    A specialized genetics laboratory evaluates the chromosome number in each sample.

  5. Reviewing results

    Your physician reviews results with you to inform embryo-selection discussions.

  6. Frozen embryo transfer

    A selected embryo is transferred in a later frozen embryo transfer cycle.

Individualized care

What to weigh when considering PGT-A

PGT-A can provide helpful information, but it has limits and is not right for everyone. It adds cost and an extra step, and results are not a promise of pregnancy. Some embryos may be reported as inconclusive.

PGT-A is different from PGT-M, which tests for a specific inherited condition, and PGT-SR, which looks at structural rearrangements. Your physician can explain which, if any, applies to you.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical or genetic advice. A consultation, sometimes with genetic counseling, is the right place to decide.

Frequently asked

Common questions

Is PGT-A required for IVF?

No. PGT-A is optional. Whether it is helpful depends on age, history, and goals, and your physician can help you decide.

What does PGT-A test for?

PGT-A checks whether an embryo has the typical number of chromosomes. It is different from tests for specific inherited conditions.

Does PGT-A guarantee a healthy pregnancy?

No. PGT-A provides information to support embryo selection, but it does not ensure pregnancy or rule out every condition.

Is the embryo biopsy safe?

The biopsy is performed by experienced laboratory staff on embryos that have reached a suitable stage. Your physician will discuss what is known about its safety.

Who might consider PGT-A?

It is often discussed with advancing age, recurrent miscarriage, prior unsuccessful IVF, or personal preference, among other reasons.

Does insurance cover PGT-A?

Coverage varies by plan. The financial team helps you understand testing fees and options before you proceed.
