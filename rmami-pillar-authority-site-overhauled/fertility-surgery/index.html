Fertility Surgery

Fertility surgery in Michigan, when a procedure can help.

Sometimes a uterine or pelvic factor affects fertility and is best addressed with a procedure. RMA of Michigan evaluates whether surgery may help and discusses minimally invasive options for conditions such as fibroids, polyps, endometriosis, or tubal factors.

In brief

What is fertility surgery?

Fertility surgery refers to procedures that address structural or anatomic factors affecting fertility, such as uterine fibroids, polyps, endometriosis, scar tissue, or fallopian tube concerns. Many procedures are minimally invasive, using small incisions or a hysteroscopic approach through the cervix. Whether surgery is helpful depends on your diagnosis and goals, and it is one option among several.

Quick answer

Not everyone needs surgery. After imaging and evaluation, your physician discusses whether a procedure may improve your situation, or whether another path such as IVF is more appropriate.

How it works

How surgical evaluation and care work

Surgery follows careful evaluation. Here is the general pathway.

  1. Evaluation and imaging

    Imaging and testing identify whether a uterine or pelvic factor may be affecting fertility.

  2. Discussing options

    Your physician explains whether a procedure may help, along with alternatives such as IVF.

  3. Minimally invasive procedure

    When appropriate, procedures use small incisions or a hysteroscopic approach for a quicker recovery.

  4. Recovery

    The team explains what to expect afterward and supports your recovery.

  5. Next steps in your plan

    Your physician connects the outcome to your broader fertility plan.

Individualized care

A measured approach to fertility surgery

Surgery is considered when a structural factor is likely affecting fertility and a procedure may help. In other cases, treatments such as IVF can work around a factor without surgery. The goal is the most effective, least burdensome path.

Many fertility-related procedures are minimally invasive, which can mean smaller incisions and a shorter recovery. Your physician will explain the specific approach, benefits, and potential risks.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical advice. A consultation is the right place to decide whether a procedure fits your plan.

Frequently asked

Common questions

When is fertility surgery recommended?

It may be considered when a structural factor such as fibroids, polyps, endometriosis, scar tissue, or a tubal concern is likely affecting fertility and a procedure may help.

Are the procedures minimally invasive?

Many are, using small incisions or a hysteroscopic approach through the cervix, which can mean a shorter recovery. Your physician explains the specific approach.

Is surgery always needed for these conditions?

No. Sometimes treatments such as IVF can work around a factor without surgery. Your physician will discuss the best path for you.

What is recovery like?

Recovery varies by procedure. The team explains what to expect and supports you afterward.

Will surgery improve my chance of pregnancy?

It depends on the diagnosis. Surgery is one option, and your physician will discuss what is realistic for your situation.

Does insurance cover fertility surgery?

Coverage varies by plan and procedure. The financial team helps verify benefits.
