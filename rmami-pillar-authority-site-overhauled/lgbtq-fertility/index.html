LGBTQ+ Family Building

LGBTQ+ fertility care in Michigan, support for every family.

RMA of Michigan welcomes LGBTQ+ individuals and couples building their families. Depending on your goals, care may include donor sperm IUI, IVF, reciprocal IVF, egg or sperm freezing, donor eggs, or gestational carrier coordination, all with respect for your identity and privacy.

In brief

How does RMA of Michigan support LGBTQ+ family building?

RMA of Michigan provides inclusive fertility care for LGBTQ+ patients. Common paths include donor sperm IUI (intrauterine insemination), IVF, reciprocal IVF (where one partner provides the eggs and the other carries the pregnancy), egg or sperm freezing, donor eggs or sperm, and gestational carrier coordination. Care is individualized to your family-building goals.

Quick answer

Your first step is a consultation to discuss your goals, any testing, donor needs, and the path that fits you. The team coordinates medical care and helps connect you with recommended legal and counseling support.

Paths to parenthood

Family-building options we support

There is no single path. Your physician helps you choose based on your goals, medical history, and any donor or carrier needs.

Reciprocal IVF

One partner provides the eggs and the other carries the pregnancy, sharing the experience between partners.

Donor sperm IUI

Prepared donor sperm is placed into the uterus around ovulation, often a first-step option.

IVF

Eggs are retrieved, fertilized in the laboratory, and an embryo is transferred or frozen.

Donor eggs or sperm

Screened donor gametes can support many family-building plans.

Gestational carrier

Medical coordination when a carrier helps build your family.

Fertility preservation

Egg, sperm, or embryo freezing to keep future options open, including before gender-affirming care.

Individualized care

Inclusive, individualized care at RMA of Michigan

Every family is different, and your plan should reflect your goals and history. The team takes time to explain options, testing, donor considerations, and timelines in clear, respectful language.

Third-party reproduction, including donor and carrier paths, involves medical, legal, and emotional planning. Legal guidance and counseling are recommended, and the team can help connect you with trusted resources.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical or legal advice. A consultation is the best place to map out your path.

Cost & coverage

Planning for cost and coverage

Costs depend on your treatment path, donor sperm or eggs, and any gestational carrier arrangement, along with your insurance benefits.

The RMA of Michigan financial team helps verify your benefits, provide a written estimate, and review financing before treatment begins, so you can plan with fewer surprises. Patients can also explore the Christen Goff IVF Grant and ask about IVF medication savings.

Frequently asked

Common questions

What is reciprocal IVF?

Reciprocal IVF is an option for some couples in which one partner provides the eggs through IVF and the other partner carries the pregnancy, allowing both to take part.

Can same-sex couples use IUI?

Yes. Donor sperm IUI is a common first-step option. Your physician will help determine whether IUI or IVF fits your goals and history.

Do we need a known or anonymous donor?

Both are possible for donor sperm or eggs. The team explains screening, options, and the recommended legal steps.

Is fertility preservation available before gender-affirming care?

Yes. Egg, sperm, or embryo freezing can be part of planning before gender-affirming care, with inclusive guidance.

What legal support is recommended?

Third-party reproduction usually involves legal guidance. The team can help connect you with experienced reproductive attorneys.

How do we get started?

Begin with a consultation. The team will discuss your goals, any testing, and a plan that fits your family.
