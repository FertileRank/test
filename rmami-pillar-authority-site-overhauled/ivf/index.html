In brief

What is IVF?

IVF is a fertility treatment in which eggs are retrieved from the ovaries, fertilized with sperm in a laboratory, and grown into embryos. An embryo is then placed in the uterus (embryo transfer), or embryos are frozen for later use. IVF may help with blocked fallopian tubes, male factor infertility, diminished ovarian reserve, endometriosis, unexplained infertility, or when genetic testing of embryos is part of the plan.

Quick answer

At RMA of Michigan in Troy, an IVF cycle typically runs about two to three weeks of stimulation and monitoring before egg retrieval, followed by fertilization, embryo culture, and a fresh or frozen transfer. Every plan is individualized.

How it works

The steps of an IVF cycle

Knowing what happens, and when, can make IVF feel less overwhelming. Here is a typical sequence — your physician will tailor the details to you.

Timelines vary, but many patients move from stimulation to embryo transfer over a few weeks, sometimes with a planned freeze-all step in between.

  1. Testing & consultation

    Ovarian reserve testing, semen analysis, and imaging help your physician design a protocol that fits your body and goals.

  2. Ovarian stimulation

    Injectable medications encourage the ovaries to mature multiple eggs, typically over about 8–12 days.

  3. Monitoring

    Morning ultrasound and bloodwork visits track follicle growth and hormone levels so medication can be adjusted.

  4. Egg retrieval

    A short, minimally invasive procedure under sedation collects the mature eggs from the ovaries.

  5. Fertilization in the lab

    Eggs and sperm are combined in the laboratory, sometimes using ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection), and embryos are cultured for several days.

  6. Embryo transfer or freeze

    A selected embryo is transferred to the uterus, or embryos are frozen for a later transfer; optional PGT-A genetic testing may be discussed first.

Is IVF right for you?

When IVF may be recommended

IVF is one of several paths to parenthood. It may be discussed when other treatments are less likely to help, or when it fits your diagnosis and goals.

Tubal & ovulation factors

Blocked or absent fallopian tubes, or ovulation challenges that have not responded to simpler treatment.

Male factor infertility

Low sperm count, motility, or morphology, often paired with ICSI to support fertilization.

Diminished ovarian reserve

Fewer available eggs, where IVF may make the most of each cycle and inform timing.

Endometriosis

When endometriosis affects egg quality, the tubes, or implantation.

Genetic testing needs

When PGT-A or PGT-M (testing for a specific inherited condition) is part of family planning.

Donor & LGBTQ+ paths

Donor eggs, donor sperm, reciprocal IVF, or a gestational carrier for many family-building goals.

Individualized care

What shapes your IVF plan at RMA of Michigan

There is no single IVF protocol that fits everyone. Your physician reviews your age, ovarian reserve, semen analysis, prior treatment response, and any uterine or genetic factors before recommending a plan.

Several factors can influence IVF outcomes, including age and ovarian reserve, embryo quality, and uterine health. Your care team will talk through what is realistic for your situation, along with alternatives such as IUI, donor options, or fertility preservation.

Frequently asked

Common questions about IVF in Michigan

How much does IVF cost in Michigan?

IVF costs vary by medication needs, genetic testing, embryo freezing, insurance benefits, and the number of cycles needed. RMA of Michigan provides a personalized financial review and written estimate before treatment begins.

How long does an IVF cycle take?

Many IVF stimulation cycles involve about 8 to 12 days of medication and monitoring before egg retrieval, but preparation, testing, embryo development, and transfer timing vary by patient.

What are the steps of an IVF cycle?

A typical IVF cycle includes testing and consultation, ovarian stimulation with medication, monitoring by ultrasound and bloodwork, egg retrieval, fertilization in the laboratory, embryo culture, and then a fresh or frozen embryo transfer.

Is ICSI always part of IVF?

ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection) is not automatically required for every IVF cycle. The care team reviews semen analysis, prior fertilization history, and clinical context before recommending it.

What is the difference between IVF and IUI?

IUI (intrauterine insemination) places prepared sperm into the uterus around ovulation and is a less involved treatment. IVF retrieves eggs, fertilizes them in the laboratory, and transfers or freezes the resulting embryos. The right option depends on diagnosis, age, and goals.

Do I need PGT-A genetic testing with IVF?

PGT-A (preimplantation genetic testing for aneuploidy) is optional. It screens embryos for chromosome number and may be discussed based on age, history, and goals. Your physician can help you decide whether it fits your plan.

Does insurance cover IVF in Michigan?

Coverage varies by plan and employer. Some plans include parts of IVF and others do not. The financial team helps verify benefits and review financing, grants, and medication savings.

Is IVF an option for LGBTQ+ couples and single parents?

Yes. IVF, reciprocal IVF, donor sperm, donor eggs, and gestational carrier coordination can support LGBTQ+ patients and single parents by choice. Care is tailored to your family-building goals.
