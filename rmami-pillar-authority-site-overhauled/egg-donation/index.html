Become an Egg Donor

Become an egg donor in Michigan and help build a family.

Egg donors give intended parents a chance at parenthood. If you are interested in donating, RMA of Michigan will walk you through eligibility, screening, and the donation cycle, with support and compensation for your time and commitment along the way.

In brief

What does becoming an egg donor involve?

Egg donation is a process in which a screened donor provides eggs that help intended parents build a family. It involves an application, medical and genetic screening, a brief course of medication with monitoring, and an egg retrieval. Donors receive compensation for their time and commitment. The team explains every step so you can make an informed decision.

Quick answer

Most donors are healthy adults within a certain age range, in good general health, and willing to complete screening. The first step is a short application, followed by screening to see whether donation is a good fit.

How it works

How to become an egg donor

The process is structured and supportive, with the team guiding you at each stage.

  1. Apply

    Complete a short application sharing basic health and background information.

  2. Screening

    If you prequalify, you complete medical, genetic, and psychological screening.

  3. Matching

    Approved donors are matched with intended parents, often anonymously.

  4. Cycle and monitoring

    A brief course of medication with ultrasound and bloodwork prepares for retrieval.

  5. Egg retrieval

    A short, minimally invasive procedure under sedation collects the eggs.

  6. Aftercare and compensation

    The team supports your recovery, and you receive compensation for your time and commitment.

Individualized care

What to know before you donate

Egg donation is a generous decision, and informed consent matters. The team will explain the time commitment, what screening involves, and what to expect during the cycle and retrieval so you can decide with confidence.

As with any procedure, there are potential risks, which the medical team reviews with you in detail. Donors are cared for throughout the process, and questions are always welcome.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical or legal advice. A conversation with the team is the best next step.

Questions to bring

  • Do I meet the general eligibility criteria?
  • What does the screening process involve?
  • What is the time commitment for a cycle?
  • What compensation and support are provided?

Frequently asked

Common questions

Who can become an egg donor?

Egg donors are generally healthy adults within a defined age range, in good general health, and willing to complete screening. An application and screening confirm whether donation is a good fit.

Is the screening process thorough?

Yes. Screening includes medical, genetic, and psychological evaluation consistent with professional guidelines, to support the health of everyone involved.

Are egg donors compensated?

Donors receive compensation for their time and commitment. Details are explained up front; see the egg donor compensation page to learn more.

What is the egg-retrieval procedure like?

It is a short, minimally invasive procedure under sedation. The team reviews what to expect and supports your recovery.

Is egg donation anonymous?

Many donations are anonymous, though arrangements vary. The team explains the options and the recommended legal steps.

How do I get started?

Start with a short application. If you prequalify, the team guides you through screening and next steps.
