Gestational Carrier

Gestational carrier care in Michigan, coordinated with care.

A gestational carrier carries a pregnancy for intended parents using an embryo created through IVF (in vitro fertilization). RMA of Michigan provides the medical coordination for intended parents and carriers, alongside the recommended legal and counseling support.

In brief

What is a gestational carrier?

A gestational carrier (sometimes called a gestational surrogate) carries a pregnancy for intended parents. The embryo is created through IVF using the intended parents' or donors' eggs and sperm, so the carrier is not genetically related to the child. The process involves medical screening, legal agreements, and careful coordination, and RMA of Michigan manages the medical side.

Quick answer

Gestational carrier journeys involve the intended parents, the carrier, the medical team, and legal counsel. The team coordinates the IVF and transfer steps while you work with an agency and attorney on matching and contracts.

How it works

How a gestational carrier journey works

Several parties work together. Here is the general medical sequence the team coordinates.

  1. Consultation and planning

    Intended parents meet with the team to plan the medical path and understand the steps involved.

  2. Carrier screening

    The gestational carrier completes medical and psychological screening consistent with guidelines.

  3. Legal agreements

    Intended parents and the carrier work with attorneys on the recommended legal agreements before treatment.

  4. Embryo creation

    An embryo is created through IVF using the intended parents' or donors' eggs and sperm.

  5. Uterine preparation

    The carrier's uterus is prepared with medication and monitoring for transfer.

  6. Embryo transfer

    A selected embryo is transferred to the carrier, with ongoing coordination of care.

Individualized care

Coordinated, careful gestational carrier care

A gestational carrier journey brings together several people and professionals. Clear communication, screening, and legal agreements help protect everyone involved and set expectations from the start.

RMA of Michigan focuses on the medical coordination, including IVF, carrier preparation, and embryo transfer, while you work with an agency and reproductive attorney on matching and contracts.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical or legal advice. A consultation is the right place to map out the path.

Frequently asked

Common questions

What is the difference between a gestational carrier and a traditional surrogate?

A gestational carrier is not genetically related to the child; the embryo is created through IVF using the intended parents' or donors' eggs and sperm. This is the standard approach today.

Who might work with a gestational carrier?

Intended parents who cannot safely carry a pregnancy, some LGBTQ+ families, and others. A consultation helps determine whether it fits.

How is a carrier screened?

Carriers complete medical and psychological screening consistent with professional guidelines, with the goal of a healthy, informed journey for everyone.

Do we need a lawyer?

Yes. Legal agreements are an essential part of a gestational carrier journey, and the team can help connect you with experienced reproductive attorneys.

Does RMA of Michigan provide the carrier or agency?

RMA of Michigan focuses on the medical coordination. Matching is typically handled through an agency, and the team can point you to resources.

How do we get started?

Begin with a consultation. The team will explain the medical path and how it coordinates with legal and agency steps.
