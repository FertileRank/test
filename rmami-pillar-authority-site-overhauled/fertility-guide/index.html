Fertility Guide

Your fertility guide, plain answers to start the journey.

Starting fertility care can feel overwhelming. This guide brings the essentials together, when to seek care, what testing involves, the main treatment options, costs, inclusive family building, and how to prepare for your first visit at RMA of Michigan.

In brief

How does fertility care work, start to finish?

Fertility care usually begins with a consultation and testing to understand factors such as ovulation, ovarian reserve, sperm, and the uterus and tubes. From there, a personalized plan may include monitoring, ovulation induction, IUI (intrauterine insemination), or IVF (in vitro fertilization), along with donor or preservation options. Cost planning and inclusive care for all families are part of the process.

Quick answer

Most people start with a consultation. Testing comes first, then a plan tailored to your diagnosis, age, and goals. You do not have to figure it out alone, the team guides each step.

Individualized care

From research to your first visit

A good first visit starts with preparation. Bring prior fertility records, recent labs, imaging, any semen analysis, a medication list, and your insurance card, along with your questions.

Treatment is rarely one-size-fits-all. The right path depends on your diagnosis, age, history, and goals, and it may change as you learn more. Education helps you take part in decisions with your care team.

This guide is educational and does not replace individualized medical advice. A consultation is the best place to turn information into a plan.

Cost & coverage

Planning for cost and coverage

Costs depend on your diagnosis, treatment type, medication, and insurance benefits, and planning should start early.

The RMA of Michigan financial team helps verify your benefits, provide a written estimate, and review financing before treatment begins, so you can plan with fewer surprises. Patients can also explore the Christen Goff IVF Grant and ask about IVF medication savings.

Financial next steps

  • Verify your insurance benefits
  • Compare financing options
  • Explore grants such as the Christen Goff IVF Grant
  • Ask about medication savings

Frequently asked

Common questions

When should I see a fertility specialist?

Consider care after 12 months of trying to conceive, or after 6 months if age 35 or older, and sooner with irregular cycles, known conditions, or recurrent pregnancy loss.

What happens at the first visit?

Your physician reviews your history and goals, discusses testing, and outlines a starting plan. Bringing records and questions helps.

What treatments are available?

Options range from ovulation induction and IUI to IVF, plus donor and preservation paths, matched to your diagnosis and goals.

How much does fertility care cost?

Costs vary widely by treatment and insurance. The financial team helps verify benefits and review financing, grants, and medication savings.

Is care inclusive of all families?

Yes. RMA of Michigan supports LGBTQ+ patients, single parents by choice, and individuals and couples building their families.

How do I get started?

Begin with a consultation. Call (248) 619-3100 or request an appointment online.
