Intrauterine Insemination (IUI)

IUI in Troy, MI, a simpler first-step fertility treatment.

IUI (intrauterine insemination) places prepared, concentrated sperm directly into the uterus around the time of ovulation to help sperm reach the egg. It is often one of the first treatments considered, and at RMA of Michigan it is planned around your diagnosis, cycle, and goals.

In brief

What is IUI?

IUI, or intrauterine insemination, is a fertility treatment in which a prepared sperm sample is placed into the uterus near ovulation using a thin, soft catheter. It is less involved and generally less costly than IVF, and may be paired with timed intercourse tracking or ovulation-stimulating medication. IUI may help with some ovulation timing needs, mild male factor findings, unexplained infertility, or when donor sperm is used.

Quick answer

An IUI is a brief, in-office procedure that is similar to a routine exam. It is typically timed to ovulation, sometimes after medication and monitoring, and many patients return to normal activities right away.

How it works

How an IUI cycle works

An IUI cycle is built around the timing of ovulation. Here is a typical sequence your physician will tailor to you.

  1. Consultation and testing

    Your physician reviews your history, ovulation pattern, semen analysis, and any prior treatment to confirm IUI is a reasonable next step.

  2. Cycle tracking or medication

    Some cycles use natural ovulation tracking; others use oral or injectable medication to support ovulation, with monitoring.

  3. Ovulation timing

    Ultrasound, bloodwork, or an ovulation predictor helps pinpoint the best window for insemination.

  4. Sperm preparation

    A partner or donor sperm sample is washed and concentrated in the laboratory to prepare it for insemination.

  5. The insemination

    Prepared sperm is placed into the uterus with a thin catheter during a short office visit.

  6. Follow-up

    A pregnancy test follows about two weeks later, and your team helps plan next steps either way.

Individualized care

What shapes your IUI plan at RMA of Michigan

IUI works best in specific situations, and it is not the right first step for everyone. Open fallopian tubes and an adequate sperm sample are usually needed, which is why testing comes first.

Several factors can influence IUI outcomes, including age, the underlying diagnosis, and whether medication is used. Many patients try a few IUI cycles before considering IVF, while others move to IVF sooner based on their diagnosis.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical advice. A consultation is the right place to review whether IUI or another option fits your situation.

Frequently asked

Common questions

What is the difference between IUI and IVF?

IUI places prepared sperm into the uterus around ovulation and is a less involved treatment. IVF retrieves eggs, fertilizes them in the laboratory, and transfers or freezes embryos. The right option depends on diagnosis, age, and goals.

Does IUI hurt?

Most patients describe IUI as similar to a routine exam, with brief, mild cramping for some. It is a short office procedure and usually does not require recovery time.

How successful is IUI?

Results vary by age, diagnosis, and whether medication is used. Your physician can discuss what is realistic for your situation and when IVF might be considered instead.

Can I use donor sperm for IUI?

Yes. Donor sperm IUI is a common path for single parents by choice, LGBTQ+ patients, and couples with male factor infertility.

How many IUI cycles should I try?

Many patients try a few cycles before reassessing, but the number depends on age and diagnosis. Your care team will help you decide when to continue or change course.

Does insurance cover IUI in Michigan?

Coverage varies by plan and employer. The RMA of Michigan financial team helps verify benefits and review options before treatment begins.
