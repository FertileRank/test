Fertility Testing and Diagnosis

Fertility testing in Troy, MI, turning uncertainty into a clear plan.

Testing is where a fertility plan begins. At RMA of Michigan, a focused evaluation looks at ovarian reserve, sperm, and the uterus and fallopian tubes to help your physician understand what is happening and recommend the next step.

In brief

What does fertility testing involve?

Fertility testing usually includes ovarian reserve testing (such as AMH, anti-Mullerian hormone, plus ultrasound), a semen analysis, and imaging of the uterus and fallopian tubes. These tests help identify factors that affect conception, such as ovulation, egg supply, sperm, or anatomy, so the care team can discuss the most useful treatment options.

Quick answer

Most testing can be completed within a cycle or two. Results are reviewed with you so you leave with a clearer understanding and a recommended next step, whether that is monitoring, IUI, IVF, or another path.

How it works

The fertility evaluation, step by step

A focused workup answers key questions quickly. Here is what a typical evaluation looks like.

  1. Consultation and history

    Your physician reviews your medical and reproductive history, cycle patterns, and goals.

  2. Ovarian reserve testing

    Bloodwork such as AMH and an ultrasound estimate the ovarian egg supply to guide planning.

  3. Semen analysis

    A partner's semen analysis evaluates sperm count, motility, and morphology.

  4. Uterine and tubal evaluation

    Imaging checks the uterus and whether the fallopian tubes are open.

  5. Hormone and health review

    Additional hormone testing is added when relevant to your situation.

  6. Results and plan

    Your team reviews results with you and recommends a next step.

Individualized care

Why testing comes first at RMA of Michigan

Testing replaces guesswork with information. Knowing your ovarian reserve, sperm findings, and uterine and tubal status helps match you to the treatment most likely to help, and avoids unnecessary steps.

No single test tells the whole story, and results are interpreted together by your physician. A normal test does not promise pregnancy, and an abnormal one rarely means there are no options.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical advice. A consultation is the right place to review your specific results.

Questions to bring

  • Which tests are appropriate for my situation?
  • What does my ovarian reserve testing suggest about timing?
  • Do both partners need testing?
  • What treatment options do my results point toward?

Frequently asked

Common questions

When should I get fertility testing?

Consider testing after 12 months of trying to conceive, or after 6 months if age 35 or older, and sooner with irregular cycles, known conditions, or recurrent pregnancy loss.

What is AMH?

AMH (anti-Mullerian hormone) is a blood test that helps estimate ovarian reserve, the relative supply of eggs, which informs treatment planning.

Does testing include my partner?

Yes. A semen analysis is an important part of the evaluation, since male factor contributes to about half of fertility challenges.

How long does testing take?

Much of the workup can be completed within a cycle or two, and your physician reviews results with you afterward.

Will testing tell me if I can get pregnant?

Testing identifies factors that affect fertility and guides options, but no test can promise pregnancy. It helps create a realistic, personalized plan.

Does insurance cover fertility testing?

Some plans cover parts of testing. The financial team helps verify your benefits before you begin.
