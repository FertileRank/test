Egg Donor Compensation

Egg donor compensation, recognizing your time and commitment.

Egg donors give an extraordinary gift, and they are compensated for their time, effort, and commitment. RMA of Michigan explains how compensation works, what is covered, and what to expect, so you can make an informed decision.

Egg donor compensation MichiganTroy, Michigan

In brief

How does egg donor compensation work?

Egg donors receive compensation that recognizes the time, effort, and commitment involved in the donation process, including screening, medication, monitoring visits, and the retrieval. Compensation is provided consistent with professional ethical guidelines. Related medical costs of the donation cycle are typically covered as well. The team explains the specifics so you understand what to expect.

Quick answer

Compensation reflects your time and commitment, not the number of eggs retrieved. The team reviews the details with you up front, along with what the donation process involves.

How it works

How compensation fits into the process

Compensation is part of a structured, supportive donor experience.

  1. Apply and prequalify

    Complete a short application and learn about the program and compensation.

  2. Screening

    Medical, genetic, and psychological screening confirms eligibility.

  3. Cycle and monitoring

    A brief course of medication with monitoring prepares for retrieval.

  4. Egg retrieval

    A short procedure under sedation collects the eggs.

  5. Compensation

    You receive compensation for your time and commitment, per program terms.

  6. Aftercare

    The team supports your recovery and answers questions.

Individualized care

Fair recognition, within ethical guidelines

Egg donation asks real time and commitment, from screening and appointments to the medication cycle and retrieval. Compensation recognizes that contribution, and the related medical costs of the cycle are typically covered.

Compensation is provided consistent with professional ethical guidelines, and it reflects your time and effort rather than outcomes. The team reviews the specifics so there are no surprises.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical or legal guidance. The team explains all terms before you proceed.

Frequently asked

Common questions

Are egg donors compensated?

Yes. Donors receive compensation that recognizes the time, effort, and commitment of the donation process, consistent with professional ethical guidelines.

Does compensation depend on the number of eggs?

No. Compensation reflects your time and commitment, not the number of eggs retrieved.

Are the medical costs of donating covered?

The related medical costs of the donation cycle are typically covered. The team explains the specifics up front.

When do I receive compensation?

Compensation is provided according to the program terms, which the team reviews with you before you begin.

What does the donation process involve?

It includes an application, screening, a brief medication cycle with monitoring, and a short egg-retrieval procedure, with aftercare and support.

How do I learn more or apply?

Visit the Become an Egg Donor page or call (248) 619-3100 to ask questions and start an application.
