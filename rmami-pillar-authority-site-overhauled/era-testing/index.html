Endometrial Receptivity Analysis (ERA)

ERA testing in Michigan, fine-tuning embryo transfer timing.

ERA (endometrial receptivity analysis) is an optional test that looks at whether the uterine lining is ready for an embryo at a given point in the cycle. It is sometimes considered for patients with prior unsuccessful transfers, and your RMA of Michigan physician will explain whether it may help.

In brief

What is the ERA test?

ERA stands for endometrial receptivity analysis. It is an optional test in which a small sample of the uterine lining is evaluated to estimate the best window for embryo transfer, sometimes called the window of implantation. ERA is most often considered for patients who have had embryo transfers that were not successful despite good-quality embryos. It is one tool, not a routine test for everyone.

Quick answer

ERA involves a mock cycle and a brief endometrial biopsy. Results may suggest adjusting transfer timing slightly. Your physician will discuss whether ERA is likely to add value in your case.

How it works

How ERA testing works

ERA is performed in a practice cycle before a future embryo transfer. Here is the general process.

  1. Discussion and planning

    Your physician reviews your transfer history and explains whether ERA may be informative.

  2. Mock cycle

    You complete a practice cycle with the same medication used to prepare for a transfer.

  3. Endometrial biopsy

    A small sample of the uterine lining is collected during a brief office procedure.

  4. Laboratory analysis

    The sample is analyzed to estimate the receptive window.

  5. Applying the results

    If indicated, transfer timing is adjusted in a future frozen embryo transfer cycle.

Individualized care

Weighing the value of ERA

ERA can be helpful for select patients, but it is not routine and the evidence continues to evolve. It adds a cycle, a procedure, and cost, so it is best considered thoughtfully with your physician.

ERA is most often discussed after embryo transfers that were not successful despite good-quality embryos, where timing may be one factor to explore among others.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical advice. A consultation is the right place to decide whether ERA fits your plan.

Questions to bring

  • Is ERA likely to add value in my case?
  • What does the mock cycle and biopsy involve?
  • How would results change my transfer plan?

Frequently asked

Common questions

Who might consider an ERA test?

It is most often considered for patients who have had embryo transfers that were not successful despite good-quality embryos. It is not a routine test for everyone.

What does the ERA test involve?

It involves a mock cycle followed by a brief endometrial biopsy, with the sample analyzed to estimate the best transfer window.

Is the biopsy painful?

Most patients experience brief cramping similar to other office procedures. Your team will explain what to expect.

Will ERA improve my chances?

ERA provides information that may refine transfer timing for select patients, but it does not promise pregnancy. Your physician will discuss whether it is likely to help.

Is ERA right for every IVF patient?

No. It is an optional test considered in specific situations, not a routine step for everyone.

Does insurance cover ERA?

Coverage varies by plan. The financial team helps you understand the fees before you proceed.
