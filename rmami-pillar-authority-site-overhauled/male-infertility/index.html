Male Factor Infertility

Male infertility care in Michigan, evaluation and clear next steps.

Male factor contributes to a large share of fertility journeys, and it is very treatable. Evaluation at RMA of Michigan starts with a semen analysis and a conversation, then connects findings to options such as IUI, IVF, ICSI, or coordination with a urologist.

In brief

What is male factor infertility?

Male factor infertility refers to sperm-related factors that can make conception more difficult, such as low sperm count, reduced movement (motility), or shape (morphology). It contributes to roughly half of fertility challenges. A semen analysis is the main first step, and many causes can be addressed with treatment, lifestyle changes, or assisted reproduction.

Quick answer

Evaluation is straightforward and usually begins with a semen analysis. Based on results, your care team discusses options, which may include IUI, IVF with ICSI, or referral to a urologist for further evaluation.

Understanding the causes

Common factors and how they are addressed

A semen analysis and history help identify contributing factors. Many are manageable with the right plan.

Sperm count

A lower number of sperm may point toward IUI, IVF, or further evaluation depending on the degree.

Motility and morphology

How sperm move and their shape can affect fertilization and guide treatment choices.

Varicocele and anatomy

Some anatomic factors can be evaluated and, in certain cases, treated by a urologist.

Hormonal factors

Hormone levels can influence sperm production and are reviewed when relevant.

Lifestyle and health

Factors such as medications, heat exposure, and overall health may be discussed.

Prior vasectomy

Options may include sperm retrieval with IVF or surgical consultation.

Individualized care

How male factor fits into a couple's plan

Fertility is a shared journey, and evaluating both partners gives the clearest picture. A semen analysis is simple and informative, and it often shapes the overall treatment plan.

When sperm factors are significant, IVF with ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection), in which a single sperm is placed into an egg, can support fertilization. Milder findings may be addressed with IUI or other steps.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical advice. A consultation, and sometimes a urology referral, is the right place to plan.

Frequently asked

Common questions

How is male infertility diagnosed?

The main first step is a semen analysis, which looks at sperm count, motility, and morphology. Hormone testing or a urology evaluation may follow based on results.

Is male infertility treatable?

Often, yes. Many factors can be addressed with treatment, lifestyle changes, or assisted reproduction such as IUI or IVF with ICSI.

What is ICSI?

ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection) places a single sperm directly into an egg during IVF and can help when sperm factors make fertilization more difficult.

Do both partners need testing?

Evaluating both partners gives the clearest picture and helps the team plan the most effective path.

Can lifestyle changes help?

Some factors, such as certain medications, heat exposure, or general health, may be discussed. Your physician can advise what is relevant for you.

Does insurance cover the evaluation?

Coverage varies by plan. The financial team helps verify benefits and review options.
