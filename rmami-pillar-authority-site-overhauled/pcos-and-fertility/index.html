PCOS and Fertility

PCOS and fertility in Michigan, supportive, individualized care.

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common reasons for irregular ovulation, and it is very manageable. RMA of Michigan helps you understand how PCOS may affect fertility and which options, from ovulation support to IVF, fit your goals.

In brief

How does PCOS affect fertility?

PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome) is a common hormonal condition that can cause irregular or absent ovulation, which makes timing conception harder. The good news is that many people with PCOS conceive with support. Options range from lifestyle guidance and ovulation induction medication to IUI (intrauterine insemination) and, when needed, IVF (in vitro fertilization).

Quick answer

Care is individualized. After testing, your physician may suggest ovulation support, sometimes with IUI, and IVF if other steps are less successful. PCOS is manageable, and a clear plan helps.

Whole-person support

Individualized care

Thoughtful, step-by-step PCOS care

PCOS looks different from person to person, so plans are individualized. Many people with PCOS respond well to ovulation support, while others benefit from IUI or IVF based on their history and other factors.

Because PCOS can affect the ovaries' response to medication, monitoring is important for both effectiveness and safety. Your physician will explain the plan and what to watch for.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical advice. A consultation is the right place to review testing and options.

Frequently asked

Common questions

Can I get pregnant with PCOS?

Many people with PCOS conceive with support. Treatment ranges from ovulation induction to IUI and IVF, tailored to your situation.

What is ovulation induction?

Ovulation induction uses oral or injectable medication, with monitoring, to encourage regular ovulation when it is irregular or absent.

Is IVF needed for PCOS?

Not always. Many people start with ovulation support, sometimes with IUI. IVF is considered when other steps are less successful or otherwise appropriate.

Why is monitoring important with PCOS?

PCOS can affect how the ovaries respond to medication, so ultrasound and bloodwork help guide effective, safe treatment.

Do lifestyle changes help PCOS fertility?

For some people, certain habits may support more regular ovulation. Your physician can discuss what is relevant for you.

Does insurance cover PCOS fertility care?

Coverage varies by plan. The financial team helps verify benefits and review options.
