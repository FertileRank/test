In brief

What fertility treatments does RMA of Michigan offer?

RMA of Michigan offers a full range of fertility treatments, including IVF (in vitro fertilization), IUI (intrauterine insemination), egg freezing, donor egg IVF, PGT-A genetic testing, ovulation induction, male infertility care, fertility surgery, and gestational carrier coordination. Care is individualized, and most patients begin with testing and a consultation to determine the most appropriate path.

Quick answer

There is no single right treatment. Your physician recommends options based on your diagnosis, age, history, and goals, often starting with less involved steps before considering IVF.

Individualized care

How to choose the right treatment

The best treatment depends on what testing reveals, your age and history, and your goals. Many patients begin with an evaluation, then ovulation support or IUI, moving to IVF if needed, while others start with IVF based on their diagnosis.

Inclusive paths, including donor sperm or eggs, reciprocal IVF, and gestational carrier care, support LGBTQ+ patients, single parents by choice, and many families. Fertility preservation is also available for planning ahead.

This page is educational and does not replace individualized medical advice. A consultation is the best place to match a treatment to your situation.

Cost & coverage

Planning for cost and coverage

Costs vary by treatment, medication, testing, and your insurance benefits, so planning should start early.

The RMA of Michigan financial team helps verify your benefits, provide a written estimate, and review financing before treatment begins, so you can plan with fewer surprises. Patients can also explore the Christen Goff IVF Grant and ask about IVF medication savings.

Financial next steps

  • Compare financing options
  • Explore grants such as the Christen Goff IVF Grant

Frequently asked

Common questions

Where do most patients start?

Many begin with fertility testing and a consultation, which guide whether monitoring, IUI, IVF, or another path is most appropriate.

Is IVF always necessary?

No. Some patients conceive with less involved treatments such as ovulation induction or IUI. IVF is recommended based on diagnosis and goals.

Do you offer donor and inclusive options?

Yes. Donor eggs, donor sperm, reciprocal IVF, and gestational carrier coordination support LGBTQ+ patients, single parents, and many families.

Can I preserve my fertility for the future?

Yes. Egg, sperm, and embryo freezing are available for planning ahead, including before medical or gender-affirming care.

How do I know which treatment is right for me?

Your physician reviews your testing, history, and goals and recommends options. The decision is made together.
