Questions & Answers

Michigan Egg Donor FAQs

Quick, honest answers to what Michigan donors ask us most.

How much do egg donors get paid in Michigan?

First-time donors typically earn $8,000–$10,000 per completed cycle. Repeat cycles are compensated at a higher rate, so dedicated Michigan donors can earn up to $47,000 in total. All medical screening, medications, and travel are covered separately—your compensation is yours to keep.

What are the requirements to donate eggs in Michigan?

You should be a healthy woman ages 21–31 with a BMI under 29, regular menstrual cycles, no nicotine or recreational drug use, and no major reproductive or genetic conditions. You’ll complete a brief medical and psychological screening, which we fully cover.

Where would my appointments take place?

Through our partner RMA of Michigan, appointments are available in the Detroit, Grand Rapids, and Ann Arbor areas. We schedule around you, and travel—including mileage and any needed lodging—is reimbursed for donors across the state.

How long does the egg donation process take?

From application to retrieval is usually 2–3 months. The active medication phase lasts about 10–12 days, and the retrieval itself is a short outpatient procedure of under 30 minutes. Most donors return to normal activities within a day or two.

Is egg donation safe, and will it affect my fertility?

Egg donation is a well-established medical procedure performed by board-certified specialists who follow ASRM safety guidelines. Research shows donation does not reduce your natural egg supply or future fertility—each cycle simply uses eggs your body would not otherwise use that month.

Is the application confidential, and is there any cost?

Your application is 100% confidential and HIPAA-secure, and there is no cost or obligation to apply. The prescreen takes about five minutes, and a Michigan donor coordinator will follow up within 1–2 business days.
