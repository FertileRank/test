How It Works

Your Journey in Four Simple Steps

Becoming an egg donor in North Carolina is straightforward—and you’re supported at every stage.

Application

Complete a quick, confidential prescreen online in about five minutes. A North Carolina donor coordinator follows up within 1–2 business days.

Screening

Complete a fully covered medical and psychological evaluation with Reproductive Specialists of the Carolinas in Charlotte, Raleigh, or Greensboro.

Matching

After a short medication phase, your retrieval is a brief outpatient procedure. Most donors are back to normal activities within a day or two.

Questions & Answers

North Carolina Egg Donor FAQs

Quick, honest answers to what North Carolina donors ask us most.

How much do egg donors get paid in North Carolina?

First-time donors typically earn $8,000–$10,000 per completed cycle. Repeat cycles are compensated at a higher rate, so dedicated North Carolina donors can earn up to $47,000 in total. All medical screening, medications, and travel are covered separately—your compensation is yours to keep.

What are the requirements to donate eggs in North Carolina?

You should be a healthy woman ages 21–31 with a BMI under 29, regular menstrual cycles, no nicotine or recreational drug use, and no major reproductive or genetic conditions. You’ll complete a brief medical and psychological screening, which we fully cover.

Where would my appointments take place?

Through our partner Reproductive Specialists of the Carolinas, appointments are available in the Charlotte, Raleigh, and Greensboro areas. We schedule around you, and travel—including mileage and any needed lodging—is reimbursed for donors across North Carolina.

How long does the egg donation process take?

From application to retrieval is usually 2–3 months. The active medication phase lasts about 10–12 days, and the retrieval itself is a short outpatient procedure of under 30 minutes. Most North Carolina donors return to normal activities within a day or two.

Is egg donation safe, and will it affect my fertility?

Egg donation is a well-established medical procedure performed by board-certified specialists who follow ASRM safety guidelines. Research shows donation does not reduce your natural egg supply or future fertility—each cycle simply uses eggs your body would not otherwise use that month.

Is the application confidential, and is there any cost?

Your application is 100% confidential and HIPAA-secure, and there is no cost or obligation to apply. The prescreen takes about five minutes, and a North Carolina donor coordinator will follow up within 1–2 business days.
