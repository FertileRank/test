Questions & Answers

Ohio Egg Donor FAQs

Quick, honest answers to what Ohio donors ask us most.

What are the requirements to donate eggs in Ohio?

To donate eggs in Ohio you should be a healthy woman ages 21–31 with a BMI under 29, regular menstrual cycles, no nicotine or recreational drug use, and no major reproductive or genetic disorders. You should live in or near Ohio (Columbus, Cincinnati, Cleveland, or Dayton) and be willing to complete a brief medical and psychological screening, which we fully cover.

How much do egg donors get paid in Ohio?

First-time donors typically earn $8,000–$10,000 per completed cycle. Repeat cycles are compensated at a higher rate, so dedicated Ohio donors can earn up to $47,000 in total. All medical screening, medications, and travel are covered separately—your compensation is yours to keep.

Where would my appointments take place in Ohio?

Through our partner SpringCreek Fertility, appointments are available in the Columbus, Cincinnati, Cleveland, and Dayton areas. We schedule around you, and travel—including mileage and any needed lodging—is reimbursed for donors across the state.

How long does the egg donation process take?

From application to retrieval is usually 2–3 months. The active medication phase lasts about 10–12 days, and the retrieval itself is a short outpatient procedure of under 30 minutes. Most donors return to normal activities within a day or two.

Is egg donation safe, and will it affect my future fertility?

Egg donation is a well-established medical procedure performed by board-certified specialists who follow ASRM safety guidelines. Research shows donation does not reduce your natural egg supply or future fertility—each cycle simply uses eggs your body would not otherwise use that month.

Is the application confidential, and is there any cost?

Your application is 100% confidential and HIPAA-secure, and there is no cost or obligation to apply. The prescreen takes about five minutes, and an Ohio donor coordinator will follow up within 1–2 business days.
