As featured on WSOC Charlotte, one mother's infertility journey reveals why choosing the right fertility specialist is the most important decision you can make.

Featured on WSOC-TV's Family Focus segment, their experience shone a light on a reality that affects one in six couples worldwide: the emotional, physical, and financial weight of infertility—and why the doctor you trust with your care can change everything.

Their turning point almost always comes down to one thing: finding the right fertility doctor.

The Infertility Journey: What So Many Families Face in Silence

Infertility is defined by the World Health Organization as the failure to achieve a pregnancy after 12 months or more of regular, unprotected intercourse. According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), approximately 1 in 5 married women aged 15 to 49 years with no prior births are unable to get pregnant after one year of trying.

Despite how common infertility is, stigma and silence continue to surround the diagnosis. Many couples suffer alone, unaware that effective treatment options exist or unsure where to begin.

Why Choosing the Right Fertility Doctor Is Critical

Not all physicians who treat infertility have the same level of training, experience, or approach. A general OB/GYN can perform initial evaluations, but complex fertility conditions often require the expertise of a reproductive endocrinologist (REI)—a physician who has completed a fellowship specifically in reproductive medicine after their OB/GYN residency.

The difference can be life-changing. Here's why your choice of fertility specialist matters so deeply:

1. Accurate Diagnosis Is the Foundation

Infertility has many potential causes, including ovulatory disorders like polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), endometriosis, blocked fallopian tubes, uterine abnormalities such as polyps or fibroids, diminished ovarian reserve, male factor infertility, and unexplained infertility. A skilled reproductive endocrinologist has the advanced training to identify subtle issues that may be missed in a standard gynecological exam. As one Charlotte-area fertility specialist noted in the WSOC segment, "Getting a diagnosis is key—not all infertility treatment involves IVF."

2. A Personalized Treatment Plan

Cookie-cutter approaches to fertility treatment can waste time, money, and emotional energy. The right fertility doctor tailors a plan to your unique medical history, test results, and goals. At RSC Fertility, treatment options range from ovulation induction and IUI to IVF, egg freezing, and third-party reproduction—and the recommended path is different for every patient.

"We pride ourselves on not being a cookie-cutter fertility clinic that rushes patients through appointments. An overbooked schedule can lead to overlooked opportunities."

— RSC Fertility Team, Charlotte, NC

3. Emotional Support and Communication

Infertility treatment is physically demanding and emotionally exhausting. The right doctor and care team don't just treat a medical condition—they support a person. Patients consistently report that feeling heard, respected, and informed by their fertility doctor is just as important as clinical outcomes.

At RSC Fertility, Dr. Matrika Johnson, who has personal experience with the fertility journey through surrogacy, brings a level of empathy that goes beyond clinical expertise. She understands how important it is to feel comfortable and confident with your specialists.

4. Expertise That Shows in Results

Board-certified reproductive endocrinologists at high-quality clinics participate in the Society for Assisted Reproductive Technology (SART) and report verified success rates. When evaluating a clinic, ask about their pregnancy rates for your age group and diagnosis, and look for transparency in reporting outcomes.

A Real Patient Story: How the Right Doctor Changed Everything

The experience of patients like Brennan Moreno, a teacher in Rock Hill, North Carolina, demonstrates how much the right fertility specialist can matter. Moreno endured years of setbacks in her journey to become a mother:

  • Multiple unsuccessful IUI attempts
  • IVF treatment with hormone injections and several egg retrievals
  • Discovery of uterine polyps requiring intervention
  • A miscarriage complicated by severe clotting issues
  • A diagnosis of placenta previa with hemorrhaging

Through every challenge, Dr. Matrika Johnson's dedication, expertise, and unwavering motivation kept Moreno going. As one of Dr. Johnson's first patients, Moreno ultimately gave birth to a healthy daughter and credits the doctor's hard work and personalized care as the reason she persevered. She shared her full story on the What the Fertility podcast.

What to Look for When Choosing a Fertility Doctor

If you're beginning your search for a fertility specialist, here are the key factors to evaluate:

  • Board certification in Reproductive Endocrinology and Infertility (REI) — This ensures your doctor has completed the highest level of specialized training in fertility medicine.
  • Personalized care philosophy — Look for a clinic that takes time with each patient rather than operating on a volume-based model. Boutique fertility clinics like RSC dedicate more time per appointment.
  • Transparent success rates — SART-reported IVF outcomes give you an objective measure of a clinic's track record.
  • Comprehensive services — A full-service fertility clinic can offer seamless transitions between treatment approaches as your needs evolve.
  • Compassionate team — From the front desk to the embryology lab, every interaction affects your experience. Read patient reviews to understand the full picture.
  • Financial transparency — Since North Carolina does not mandate fertility insurance coverage, ask about financing options, payment plans, and package pricing upfront.

The Fight for Fertility Coverage in North and South Carolina

The cost of fertility treatment remains one of the biggest barriers for families in the Carolinas. As highlighted in WSOC's coverage of local advocacy efforts, neither North Carolina nor South Carolina currently require insurance companies to cover infertility treatment—despite 19 states having mandatory fertility coverage laws on the books.

Advocates like Fort Mill mother Caryn Rich, who spent hundreds of thousands of dollars on IVF and endured five miscarriages before completing her family, are working with organizations like RESOLVE: The National Infertility Association to push for legislative change.

Fertility Treatment Cost Considerations

  • A single IVF cycle in the United States can range from $15,000 to $30,000
  • Many patients require multiple cycles before achieving a successful pregnancy
  • IUI treatment is generally less expensive, ranging from $500 to $4,000 per cycle
  • Fertility medications can add $3,000 to $5,000 or more per cycle
  • RSC Fertility offers financial counseling and flexible financing to help patients manage costs

When Should You See a Fertility Specialist?

Medical guidelines from the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) recommend consulting a fertility specialist in these circumstances:

  • Under 35: After 12 months of trying to conceive without success
  • Ages 35–39: After 6 months of trying
  • Age 40 and older: Seek evaluation promptly, as fertility declines more rapidly
  • Known risk factors: If you have irregular or absent periods, a history of endometriosis, PCOS, recurrent miscarriage, prior cancer treatment, or known male factor issues, seek evaluation sooner

Early evaluation can save valuable time. A comprehensive fertility assessment typically includes hormone testing, an ultrasound evaluation, a semen analysis for the male partner, and may include additional imaging or diagnostic procedures.
